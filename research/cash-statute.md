# Research: Does Fla. Stat. § 627.748 impose an electronic-payment mandate?

Resolves [yeride-website#32](https://github.com/yeapptech/yeride-website/issues/32) (wayfinder map #30).
Verifies product obligation #5 in `yeride-brand/docs/positioning.md` before the rider copy
**"Card or cash. / Tarjeta o efectivo."** ships on the redesigned site.

> **This is research, not legal advice.** It reflects the primary statute text as read on
> 2026-07-30 and should inform, not replace, counsel review where flagged below.

**Primary source:** Fla. Stat. § 627.748, official Florida Legislature site (Online Sunshine),
[full text](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0627/Sections/0627.748.html)
— page header **"The 2025 Florida Statutes"** (current published edition), fetched live 2026-07-30.
History line (verbatim): *"History.—s. 1, ch. 2017-12; s. 86, ch. 2018-110; s. 1, ch. 2020-87."*
— enacted 2017 (CS/HB 221), last amended by ch. 2020-87 (2020).

## 1. Does the statute mandate electronic/cashless payment? **No.**

The entire section (all 18 subsections) was read verbatim. **No provision requires fares to be
paid electronically, through the app/digital network, or by card, and no provision prohibits
cash.** The "payment only through the digital network" clause found in some other states' TNC
acts does not appear anywhere in the Florida statute. The words "cash," "credit card," and
"electronic payment" never appear; "payment" appears only in the insurance subsections
((7)(i), (8)(b) — medical-payments coverage, claim payments).

## 2. Payment-adjacent provisions, quoted

**§ 627.748(4) — fare transparency (in full):**

> "If a fare is collected from a rider, the TNC must disclose to the rider the fare or fare
> calculation method on its website or within the online-enabled technology application service
> before the beginning of the prearranged ride. If the fare is not disclosed to the rider before
> the beginning of the prearranged ride, the rider must have the option to receive an estimated
> fare before the beginning of the prearranged ride."

It says "If a fare **is collected**" — it regulates *disclosure*, and is silent on the
*medium* of payment.

**§ 627.748(6) — electronic receipt:**

> "Within a reasonable period after the completion of a ride, a TNC shall transmit an electronic
> receipt to the rider on behalf of the TNC driver which lists: (a) The origin and destination of
> the ride; (b) The total time and distance of the ride; and (c) The total fare paid."

The *receipt* must be electronic; nothing says the *payment* must be. A cash fare can be
recorded and receipted electronically.

**§ 627.748(13) — prohibited conduct (the closest thing to a constraint):**

> "(a) A TNC driver may not accept a ride for compensation other than by a rider arranged
> through a digital network.
> (b) A TNC driver may not solicit or accept street hails."

This mandates digital **prearrangement of the ride**, not digital payment. A ride booked in-app
and paid in cash satisfies (13)(a) on its plain text.

**§ 627.748(17) — preemption** (note: (15) is records, not preemption):

> "TNCs, TNC drivers, TNC vehicles, luxury ground TNCs, luxury ground TNC drivers, and luxury
> ground TNC vehicles are governed exclusively by state law… A county, municipality, special
> district, airport authority, port authority, or other local governmental entity or subdivision
> may not: … 2. Subject a TNC, a TNC driver, a TNC vehicle… to any rate, entry, operation, or
> other requirement of the county, municipality…"

So a Florida city or county also cannot impose its own cashless-payment mandate on a TNC —
payment method is left to state law, which is silent, i.e. permissive.

## 3. Do the definitions constrain payment method? No — prearrangement ≠ payment.

- **(1)(a):** "'Digital network' means any online-enabled technology application service,
  website, or system offered or used by a transportation network company which enables the
  **prearrangement of rides**…" — the network's defined function is arranging rides, not
  processing payment.
- **(1)(b):** "'Prearranged ride' means the provision of transportation by a TNC driver to a
  rider, beginning when a TNC driver accepts a ride requested by a rider through a digital
  network… The term does not include a taxicab or street hail service…" — defined by how the
  ride *begins*, not how it is paid.
- **(1)(e):** "'Transportation network company' or 'TNC' means an entity operating in this state
  pursuant to this section using a digital network to connect a rider to a TNC driver, who
  provides prearranged rides."
- **(1)(c):** "'Rider' means an individual who uses a digital network to connect with a TNC
  driver in order to obtain a prearranged ride…"

Every digital-network reference concerns connection/prearrangement. **Interpretation (labeled
as such):** so long as every ride is requested and accepted through the app, collecting the
fare in cash at ride's end does not take the service outside these definitions. One
definitional edge: (1)(b) excludes services "in which the driver receives a fee that does not
exceed the driver's cost" — irrelevant to a commercial fare, cash or card.

**Agency guidance / enforcement:** searches for FLHSMV, DFS, and OIR guidance or enforcement on
TNCs accepting cash found **nothing authoritative on point** — no Florida agency guidance,
rule, or enforcement action addressing cash acceptance by TNCs appears to exist. DFS's role
under § 627.748(12)(e)–(f) is limited to receiving biennial CPA examination reports on
subsections (8) (insurance) and (12) (background checks) — payment method is outside that
audit's scope. (Corroboration only, not authority: Uber has itself offered cash payment in some
markets; no Florida action against cash TNC fares surfaced.)

## 4. Bottom line

**Yes — under the current text of Fla. Stat. § 627.748, a Florida TNC may lawfully accept cash
fares.** The "Card or cash / Tarjeta o efectivo" claim is consistent with the statute, provided
the TNC:

1. Arranges every ride through its digital network — drivers may not take street hails or
   off-app rides for compensation ((13)(a)–(b));
2. Discloses the fare or fare-calculation method in-app/on its website before the ride begins,
   or offers an estimated fare ((4));
3. Transmits an **electronic receipt** after every ride, including cash rides, showing
   origin/destination, time/distance, and total fare paid ((6));
4. Keeps individual ride records for at least 1 year ((15)(a)).

**Caveats worth a lawyer's review** (flagged, not opined on):

- (a) Cash handling raises non-§ 627.748 issues — driver-safety policies, tax reporting,
  money-services considerations if the TNC intermediates driver cash remittances;
- (b) Airport/seaport operating agreements under (17)(b) can impose their own contractual terms;
- (c) Statutes get amended — re-verify before each campaign cycle (text read is the 2025
  edition; last substantive amendment was 2020).

None of these stem from a cashless mandate in § 627.748, because there isn't one.
